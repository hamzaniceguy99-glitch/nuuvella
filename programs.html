The programs, in detail

Four programs of 6 to 12 weeks, worked on your own family and your own brick walls.

Program 01 · Built to hold up

Starting your tree

Start with the living. We plan interviews with older relatives before those memories are gone, mine the documents already in your family's drawers, then work backwards generation by generation — proving each step rather than guessing. Citations from the start save enormous pain later.

  • 10 weeks
  • Complete beginners
  • 3 hours a week
  • One curious relative

Start this program

Weeks 1–2 · The living

Interview questions and recording family memory properly.

Weeks 3–4 · Home sources

Documents, photographs and what's in the attic.

Weeks 5–8 · Working back

Four documented generations, one step at a time.

Weeks 9–10 · Organizing

A tree, a filing system and citations that make sense later.

Weeks 1–3 · Census

Reading them properly, including the columns everyone skips.

Weeks 4–6 · Vital records

Birth, marriage and death, and how to order originals.

Weeks 7–9 · Migration

Ship lists, naturalization and following people across borders.

Weeks 10–12 · Handwriting

Old scripts and abbreviations, practiced on your documents.

Program 02 · Finding the document

Records & archives

Most answers exist in records that aren't indexed the way you'd expect. We cover the major record sets, searching for misspellings and variants, requesting from county and state offices, and reading old handwriting — which is a learnable skill, not a talent.

  • 12 weeks
  • Some basics
  • 4 hours a week
  • A subscription helps

Start this program

Program 03 · What matches actually mean

DNA, sensibly

DNA is powerful and routinely over-read. We work on interpreting shared centimorgans honestly, grouping matches to identify which branch they come from, and combining DNA with documents — because alone it rarely proves a specific relationship. We do not interpret health or medical results; that belongs with a doctor or genetic counselor.

  • 8 weeks
  • Have taken a test
  • 3 hours a week
  • Your match list

Program 04 · Before it's lost again

Writing it down

Research nobody reads is a hobby, not a legacy. We turn your findings into short written stories about specific people, place them in historical context, and produce something shareable — a printed book, a document, or a set of stories sent to the family.

  • 4 hours a week
